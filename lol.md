# WARD THIRTEEN — FULL 3D HORROR GAME DEVELOPMENT PROMPT

Act as a senior AAA horror game developer, gameplay programmer, level designer, environmental artist, lighting artist, and sound designer.

Build a complete, playable, immersive FIRST-PERSON 3D survival horror game called:

# WARD THIRTEEN

Tagline: "Ward 13 was abandoned years ago, but something inside never left."

## 1. CORE VISION

Create a realistic, atmospheric, deeply unsettling 3D horror game set inside an abandoned hospital at night.

The game must feel like a real horror game made by a small professional game studio.

IMPORTANT:
- Absolutely avoid the generic AI-generated game aesthetic.
- No cartoon graphics.
- No colorful fantasy environments.
- No random floating objects.
- No excessive visual effects.
- No generic AI-looking textures.
- No unnecessary UI elements.
- No overly bright lighting.
- No robotic character animations.
- No repetitive jumpscares every few seconds.

Everything should feel grounded, believable, imperfect, and physically present.

Use realistic proportions, natural imperfections, detailed materials, and cinematic environmental storytelling.

## 2. GAME INTRODUCTION

Start the player OUTSIDE the abandoned hospital.

The player spawns directly in front of the hospital's main entrance gate.

Create:

- Broken boundary walls.

- Broken hospital windows.
- Old ambulance parked near the entrance.
- A security cabin with a broken light.
- Papers scattered across the entrance.
- Rusty chains and an old locked gate.

The hospital should look genuinely abandoned, not artificially decorated.

## 21. ADVANCED HORROR EVENTS, SHADOW MOVEMENTS & FUNCTIONAL ELEVATORS

Create an unpredictable, cinematic horror system that makes WARD THIRTEEN genuinely terrifying.

The horror must feel natural, unexpected, and intelligently timed. Do not rely on repetitive jumpscares.

### 1. SHADOW FIGURES & UNEXPLAINED MOVEMENTS

Add realistic paranormal events throughout the hospital.

Examples:

- When the player walks down a dark corridor, a human-like shadow suddenly moves from LEFT TO RIGHT at the far end.
- A dark silhouette quickly disappears behind a doorway.
- The player turns toward a room and sees someone standing there for a split second.
- A shadow moves across the wall even though nobody is nearby.
- Something quickly runs across the corridor ahead of the player.
- A silhouette disappears behind a hospital bed.
- A dark figure passes behind a glass window.
- A shadow appears at the end of the hallway and vanishes when approached.
- An empty wheelchair suddenly moves slightly.
- A distant figure walks across the corridor before disappearing around a corner.

Make these events feel physically believable.

### 2. CINEMATIC CROSSING EVENTS

Create specially designed horror encounters.

Example sequence:

1. The player walks through a long, dimly lit hospital corridor.
2. A flickering light creates an unsettling atmosphere.
3. Just before the player reaches the middle of the corridor, a human-like figure suddenly runs from LEFT TO RIGHT across the player's path.
4. The figure disappears behind a doorway.
5. The player hears fast footsteps moving away.
6. The player investigates, but the hallway is completely empty.

Create variations:
- Left to right.
- Right to left.
- Behind the player.
- Across a distant doorway.
- Between two hospital beds.
- Through a glass window.
- At the end of a hallway.

Use natural animation and smooth movement. Avoid sliding or teleporting-looking character models.

### 3. UNEXPECTED SHADOW EVENTS

Implement an event-based horror director.

It should trigger encounters based on:
- Player location.
- Time spent exploring.
- Player movement.
- Whether the player is looking toward a particular area.
- Mission progression.
- Previous horror encounters.

Do not trigger events constantly.

Sometimes allow several minutes of complete silence.

The player should never know when something will happen.

### 4. ELEVATOR SYSTEM — FULLY FUNCTIONAL

Implement actual working elevators throughout the hospital.

Include:
- Ground floor.
- First floor.
- Second floor.
- Basement.

Elevator features:

- Functional call buttons.
- Floor selection buttons.
- Opening and closing doors.
- Realistic elevator movement.
- Floor indicator display.
- Elevator motor sounds.
- Button illumination.
- Emergency lighting.
- Slight mechanical shaking.
- Realistic arrival bell.
- Elevator interior lighting.

The player must be able to enter, exit, and travel between accessible floors.

Elevators must be physically integrated into the hospital architecture, with proper collision and navigation.

### 5. ELEVATOR-SPECIFIC HORROR EVENTS

Create unpredictable elevator encounters.

Examples:

**EVENT A — WRONG FLOOR**

The player selects the first floor, but the elevator stops at an abandoned, unlisted floor. The doors open slowly, revealing a completely dark corridor.

**EVENT B — SOMEONE OUTSIDE**

The elevator doors begin opening. A dark figure stands outside for a moment before suddenly disappearing.

**EVENT C — UNEXPECTED ARRIVAL**

While the player is alone inside the elevator, the floor indicator changes unexpectedly. The elevator stops, but nobody enters.

**EVENT D — FLICKERING LIGHTS**

The elevator lights flicker briefly. When they turn back on, something in the elevator appears to have changed.

**EVENT E — FOOTSTEPS**

The player hears footsteps approaching from outside while the elevator doors remain closed.

These events should be rare, atmospheric, and different on each playthrough.

### 6. CCTV AND SHADOW CONNECTION

Connect paranormal events to the CCTV system.

Sometimes:
- A shadow appears on Camera 03 but not in the actual corridor.
- A figure moves between cameras.
- A figure stands behind the player in a CCTV feed.
- A camera briefly loses signal when something approaches.
- The player sees an unexplained movement in a previously empty room.

### 7. AUDIO AND ANIMATION

Every encounter should have appropriate sound design.

Use:
- Fast footsteps.
- Distant running.
- Brief metallic impacts.
- Door slams.
- Electrical buzzing.
- Elevator motor sounds.
- Subtle breathing.
- Sudden silence.

Animations must look natural, with proper human movement, acceleration, turning, and disappearing behind actual environmental geometry.

### 8. IMPORTANT HORROR DESIGN RULES

- No cheap jumpscares every minute.
- No random monsters appearing directly in front of the camera.
- No obvious scripted-looking movements.
- No unrealistic teleportation.
- No excessive screen shaking.
- No loud sounds without environmental context.

Make encounters rare enough that players question whether they actually saw something.

**FINAL GOAL:**

Make players afraid to walk down empty corridors, hesitate before entering elevators, and constantly check behind themselves.

The hospital should feel like something is moving around even when the player cannot see it.



## 22. INTELLIGENT ENEMY AI, PLAYER DETECTION & SURVIVAL MECHANICS

Create a highly realistic enemy AI system that makes the player feel hunted throughout WARD THIRTEEN.

The enemy must not behave like a simple NPC that randomly walks around. It should feel intelligent, unpredictable, and dangerous.

### 1. ENEMY BEHAVIOUR

The entity should have different behaviour states:

- **Wandering:** Slowly explores hospital corridors.
- **Observing:** Watches the player from a distance.
- **Investigating:** Moves toward sounds made by the player.
- **Searching:** Checks nearby rooms after losing sight of the player.
- **Hunting:** Actively follows the player.
- **Chasing:** Runs toward the player when detected.
- **Disappearing:** Occasionally retreats into darkness.

### 2. PLAYER DETECTION

The entity should detect the player through:

- Line of sight.
- Distance.
- Running footsteps.
- Opening doors.
- Dropping objects.
- Using certain noisy equipment.

Crouching and moving slowly should reduce the chance of detection.

The enemy should not magically know the player's location.

### 3. HIDING MECHANICS

Allow players to hide inside:

- Wardrobes.
- Storage cabinets.
- Under hospital beds.
- Behind large furniture.
- Inside selected dark rooms.

When hiding, reduce visibility and movement sounds.

The enemy should sometimes investigate hiding places rather than automatically ignoring them.

### 4. REALISTIC CHASE SEQUENCES

During certain missions, trigger carefully designed chase sequences.

Include:
- Heavy footsteps approaching.
- Flickering lights.
- Doors slamming.
- The enemy appearing at corridor intersections.
- Emergency lights activating.
- The player needing to find an escape route.

The chase must feel intense but fair.

### 5. PLAYER SURVIVAL

Implement:
- Limited flashlight battery.
- Collectible batteries.
- Limited inventory capacity.
- Hiding.
- Crouching.
- Sprint stamina.
- Checkpoints.
- Health and damage feedback where appropriate.

### 6. DYNAMIC HORROR DIRECTOR

Create a system that controls horror intensity.

If the player has experienced several frightening events, allow a period of calm.

If the player has been exploring safely for a long time, introduce a subtle encounter.

Never make every encounter predictable.

### 7. IMPORTANT

The enemy must use actual navigation and collision-aware movement.

It must not walk through walls, teleport directly behind the player, or behave like a floating model.

Use believable animations, footstep audio, turning behaviour, and environmental awareness.

The game should create fear through anticipation, uncertainty, and the possibility of being discovered.

**FINAL GOAL: Make the player feel that they are not exploring an abandoned hospital alone. Something is actively hunting them.**
